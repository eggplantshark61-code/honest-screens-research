Learn

Research concepts, in plain English

Short, evergreen explainers for the ideas behind the reports — useful whether you follow the research or are just learning the vocabulary.

Analyst price target

A research firm's estimate of where a stock might trade over roughly a year. It's an opinion, not a promise — targets are often wrong. Every target here is cross-checked across several independent sources and averaged, so no single feed drives the number.

Stock screening

Filtering the whole market with explicit, repeatable rules — analyst rating, size, liquidity, target upside, trend versus the 50- and 200-day moving averages — to surface a shortlist worth a closer look. A screen is a starting point, never a conclusion.

Technical & Elliott Wave

Technical analysis reads price structure — support, resistance, moving averages, momentum. Elliott Wave interprets price as repeating impulsive and corrective patterns. Both are subjective lenses, so any read here is one interpretation, not a forecast.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Quick answers to the questions I get most about the research and how to read it.

What is an analyst price target?

It's a research firm's estimate of where a stock could trade over a set horizon, usually 12 months. It is an opinion, not a guarantee, and estimates frequently prove wrong. On this site each target is cross-checked across several independent sources and averaged.

How does a stock screen work?

A screen filters the market using explicit, repeatable rules — analyst rating, company size, liquidity, target upside, and trend relative to moving averages — to surface a shortlist of candidates for deeper review.

Is this website investment advice?

No. Everything here is for information and education only. It is not personalized advice or a recommendation, and I am not a registered investment adviser. Always do your own research and consider consulting a licensed professional.

What is Elliott Wave analysis?

A technical framework that interprets price movement as repeating impulsive and corrective wave patterns. It is inherently subjective, so any count here is presented as one interpretation, not a forecast.
